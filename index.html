            Review day

            Presentation, interview and communication task

            What happens on the day, what ICE says about each part, and practical advice. Facts from ICE's guidance are marked ICE with a page number; everything under "Our advice" is our own suggestion.

            The day at a glance (Chartered review)

            The presentation

            ICE
            • 15 minutes. Reviewers may stop you if you go over. p. 13
            • It should expand on one aspect of your report. It should not cover all the attributes, or summarise your CV or report. p. 13
            • Visual aids are encouraged. In person, no larger than A3; a laptop is allowed but no power supply is provided. Online, share your screen on Teams. p. 13

            Our advice

            • Pick the decision you're proudest of, where you made the call. Structure it as problem, options, your decision and why, result, and what you'd do differently.
            • Aim for 13 minutes, so nerves or a question don't push you past 15.
            • Use drawings, sections and photos rather than text-heavy slides. Show numbers you can talk to.
            • Rehearse aloud with a timer at least five times, and once in front of someone who'll interrupt you.
            • Online: test screen sharing on Teams beforehand and have a PDF backup ready.

            The interview

            ICE
            • Two peer reviewers assess you; a third may join for specialist knowledge, and an observer may sit in without taking part. pp. 4, 12-13
            • The presentation and interview together last up to 75 minutes for a Chartered review (60 for Incorporated). p. 13
            • Reviewers confirm you've reached the required level in all the attributes. Where your report is light on an attribute, expect specific questions on it. p. 13
            • Phones off before the interview starts; recording isn't allowed. p. 13

            Our advice

            • Answer in the first person and give a real example for each claim: the situation, what you did, and the result.
            • Know every number in your report. If one is wrong, correct it before they find it.
            • If you don't know, say so. Then say what you do know, how you'd find out, and how you'd keep things safe meanwhile. Never bluff.
            • It's fine to pause, ask them to repeat a question, or sketch to explain.
            • Revise the Code of Conduct, CPD, H&S and current affairs: the attributes cover them all. The flashcards help.

            The written communication task

            ICE
            • Taken after the interview: 90 minutes for a Chartered review, 60 for Incorporated. pp. 13-14
            • Chartered: choose one of two scenarios on current industry issues, written in a given style such as a blog post. Incorporated: answer one of two questions as a technical memo or note. pp. 13-14
            • You may use two sides of A4 of reference notes, but not copy and paste from them. No internet, search engines or AI tools. p. 27
            • Upload it as PDF or Word within 15 minutes of the end time. In person, bring your own laptop. pp. 14, 27
            • For Chartered, reviewers look at whether you can digest the issue, organise your thoughts and present them logically and clearly. p. 13

            Our advice

            • Split the 90 minutes: about 10 to choose and plan, 65 to write, 15 to check. Planning is what makes it read clearly.
            • Write for the audience and style you're given. A blog post for the public reads differently from a note to a client.
            • Use a simple structure: the issue, why it matters, the options or arguments, your view, and what should happen next.
            • Bring in a current example and, where it fits, sustainability, safety and cost.
            • Fill your two A4 sides with facts and figures on likely topics (from the News page), not pre-written paragraphs.
            • Practise at least three timed tasks, typed, with no internet.
            On the day ICE p. 24
            • Dress professionally and bring ID.
            • In person: arrive at least 30 minutes early to register. Bring a copy of your submission, your visual aids, laptop and power cable, and your two A4 sides.
            • Online: a reliable connection, a laptop or PC with webcam and headset, power, and a private room. Have your submission open, paper and pen ready, and some water.
            After the review ICE pp. 14-15
            • You'll get your result by email no later than 6 weeks after the review.
            • If you're unsuccessful, you'll get the reviewers' comments. An appeal must reach ICE within two months of the date on your result letter.
            • To try again, you go through the full application process.

              How the feedback works

              The reviewer is told what ICE reviewers look for under each attribute, the extra the chartered level asks for, and the common reasons reports fall short: writing as "we", no numbers, reflections that don't say what changed, claims that go beyond your role, and figures that don't add up. It can be wrong. Treat it as a second pair of eyes, not a verdict, and always take your supervisor's or mentor's advice over it.
